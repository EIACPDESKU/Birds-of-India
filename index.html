Bird Species Information

Red Spurfowl

Galloperdix spadicea

The Red Spurfowl (Galloperdix spadicea) is a terrestrial bird species native to the Indian subcontinent. It primarily inhabits dry deciduous forests, scrublands, and hilly regions with dense undergrowth. The bird is often found near water sources and prefers habitats with abundant cover for foraging and protection. Its range includes lowland and foothill forests, often venturing into cultivated areas and gardens near forest edges.

Red-wattled Lapwing

Vanellus indicus

The Red-wattled Lapwing (Vanellus indicus) is commonly found across South and Southeast Asia, inhabiting open fields, grasslands, riverbanks, wetlands, and agricultural lands. It prefers flat terrains near water sources such as ponds, lakes, and irrigation channels but can also adapt to drier areas and human-modified landscapes. Known for its distinctive loud calls, this bird is often seen foraging for insects and small invertebrates in open habitats.

Least Concern

Mottled Wood-Owl

Strix ocellata

The Mottled Wood-Owl (Strix ocellata) is a nocturnal bird species native to the Indian subcontinent, typically inhabiting deciduous and semi-evergreen forests, wooded areas, and groves near human settlements. It thrives in habitats with mature trees, often in open woodlands, gardens, and rural areas, avoiding dense forests. This owl is known for its deep, resonant calls and relies on its camouflage to roost in tree canopies during the day.

Least Concern

Spotted Dove

Spilopelia chinensis

The Spotted Dove (Spilopelia chinensis) is a widespread bird found across South and Southeast Asia, favoring open woodlands, gardens, parks, farmlands, and urban areas. It thrives in habitats with scattered trees and shrubs, often near human settlements. Known for its soft cooing calls and distinctive spotted neck pattern, this dove forages on the ground for seeds, grains, and small invertebrates, frequently seen in pairs or small groups.

Least Concern
